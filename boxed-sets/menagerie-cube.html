Menagerie Cube

Draft · v0.1 · six-player cube, 270 slots

A six-player draft cube about mad scientists combining creatures.

Draft v0.1

The rules and the 270-slot list are complete. The list may still change.

The cube

Every player is a mad scientist building monsters. Creatures combine with each other through augment and hosts, mutate, bestow, and the Un-set stuck-together creatures (B.F.M., S.N.O.T., Grusilda). Avatar: The Last Airbender hybrid animals (Tiger-Seal, Platypus-Bear, Cat-Gator) and other non-Human creatures are the bodies the experiments are built on; they already read like Unstable's augment creatures.

The cube is drafted by 6 players with 3 packs of 15 cards each (270 pick slots), followed by 1v1 matches. Packs are random: shuffle all 270 slots together and deal 18 packs of 15. A six-player draft uses the whole cube, so every augment and every host is in every draft.

Un-cards follow the Un-cards guideline: cards that bend the game's rules are in; cards that count things in the room or need physical stunts are out.

Archetypes

The cube has three archetypes: augment, mutate, and bestow. They are defined by mechanic, not by color pair, and each appears in every color.

The core: what Surgeon General Commander cares about

Surgeon General Commander draws a card whenever you augment, enchant, or mutate a creature you control, and taps for any color. It fits any deck and is the draft's chase card. Its three triggers are the three archetypes:

Archetype What the deck does Cards in the list
Augment Play hosts, then combine augments onto them. 19 augments (14 different cards), 31 hosts (20 different), and 8 support cards (Clever Combo, Dr. Julius Jumblemorph, Really Epic Punch, Success!, Teacher's Pet)
Mutate Stack mutate creatures onto non-Human creatures. 34 mutate creatures, 5 support cards
Bestow Enchant your creatures with bestow creatures. The goal is enchanting creatures, not an enchantment deck. 19 bestow creatures

Hybrid animals are the shared bodies: non-Human creatures to mutate onto and bestow onto. Hosts are a separate card type; augments can only combine with hosts.

Other build-arounds: Dr. Julius Jumblemorph (finds augments), Grusilda, Monster Masher (combined and enchanted creatures get menace; combines creatures from graveyards), Diligent Zookeeper (rewards many creature types), Morophon, and Chromanticore.

What to expect

  • Most decks combine creatures. Hosts, mutate targets, and bestow targets are common enough that any drafter who wants to combine can.
  • Almost every creature is non-Human, because mutate can only target non-Humans.
  • Games are slower than in a usual cube. The fixing is four copies of each Vivid land, and they enter tapped. Two- and three-drops are common, so an aggressive deck can still punish a slow start.
  • Play any colors. Every mechanic appears in every color, and the Vivid lands make a third or fourth color practical.
  • Removal is bites, fights, edicts, Auras, damage, and turning a creature into a small animal. There are no counterspells.
  • Bring answers for one huge creature. A combined creature holds two or more cards, and one removal spell trades for all of them.

Base rules and cube changes

The base ruleset is Booster-draft Cube, which covers the draft, deck construction, and games. Decks are at least 40 cards, games are 1v1 and start at 20 life, and normal Magic rules apply. Basic lands come from a separate land station.

This cube adds three rules and has one rulings sheet:

  • MC1 (addition): bundled picks. Drafting the S.N.O.T. in the packs also takes the rest of the stack from a side pile.
  • MC2 (rulings): how augment, mutate, bestow, and the Un-cards interact.
  • MC3 (addition): Crafty Octopus comes with a preset Contraption deck.
  • MC4 (addition): B.F.M. is not drafted; any player may cast it from outside the game.

MC1. Bundled picks

Some cards only work together. Each bundle has one card in the cube's packs. The rest of the bundle waits in a side pile next to the draft. When you draft the pack card, take the rest of its bundle from the side pile and add it to your drafted pool.

Bundle Card in the pack Side pile
S.N.O.T. stack One S.N.O.T. The other three copies. The stack is four S.N.O.T.s in all.

A bundle fills one pick slot, so packs stay at 15 cards. All slot counts on this page count each bundle once.

After the draft, each card in a bundle is an ordinary drafted card. You may play any of them, all of them, or none.

MC2. Rulings sheet

These rulings cover the places where the cube's mechanics meet. Each one says what it rests on. Augment and host are not in the Comprehensive Rules, so Wizards' Unstable and Unsanctioned FAQs and the card rulings are the authority for them. Rule numbers are from the Comprehensive Rules effective November 6, 2026.

  1. Mer Man can't be mutated onto. Mutate targets a non-Human creature (CR 702.140a), and Mer Man is a Human Fish. Augmenting it changes nothing: "Human Fish" stays visible on the combined creature. Diligent Zookeeper gives it no bonus either, because Zookeeper counts only non-Human creatures.
  2. A combined creature has the creature types you can see. The augment card covers the words "Host Creature" and leaves the host's creature types showing. The combined creature has the augment's types and the host's types, and it is no longer a host. A type printed on both cards counts once: Half-Kitten, Half-Adorable Kitten is a Cat, so Diligent Zookeeper gives it +1/+1. Wizards' FAQ: "The game can see basically anything visible ... Anything covered up in the augment process doesn't count."
  3. A mutated creature has the creature types of its top card only (CR 731.2a). It has the abilities of every card in the pile (CR 702.140e).
  4. You may mutate onto a combined creature. A host and its augment "form a new single creature" (Unstable FAQ). It is a legal target if it is non-Human and you own it (CR 702.140a).
  5. You may augment a mutated creature only while a host card is on top of the pile. Augment can only target a host (ruling on Half-Kitten, Half-). With a mutate card on top, the creature has that card's type line (CR 731.2a) and is not a host. A host takes one augment at most.
  6. A mutated creature that is flickered or exiled comes back as separate creatures. Every card in the pile leaves together, and whatever returns them returns each one by itself (CR 731.3, 731.3c). That is what Ordinary Pony does to a mutated creature.
  7. A combined creature that is flickered or exiled comes back as the host alone. Both cards leave: "What happens to the creature happens to both cards" (Unstable FAQ). The host returns and its enters ability triggers. The augment card can't be on the battlefield without a host: "If you somehow put a card with augment onto the battlefield without actually augmenting it, it will head to the graveyard." The FAQ has no flicker example; this follows from those two answers.
  8. Bestow counts as enchanting for Surgeon General Commander. A creature cast with bestow is an Aura (CR 702.103b), and an Aura "enchants" what it is attached to (CR 303.4b). You draw when the Aura spell resolves and becomes attached to a creature you control (CR 608.3c). A countered spell enchants nothing. A bestow spell whose target is gone resolves as a creature and enchants nothing (CR 702.103e). Neither draws a card.
  9. Underdome's colored mana pays any cost that comes from a silver-bordered or acorn card: casting it, its augment cost, and its abilities. It does not pay for black-bordered cards, including their mutate and bestow costs. The Unsanctioned FAQ defines the cost as "any cost imposed by a card with a silver border," and the Unfinity release notes say acorn symbols and silver borders mean the same thing.
  10. In a mutate pile, a host and its augment count as one card. They stay joined whether the mutate card goes over or under them. With the mutate card on top, the creature has that card's name, types, power and toughness, and it also has the combined pair's ability. With the pair on top, it is still the combined creature, and it also has the mutate card's abilities. Official text has no answer; this is our ruling, and it follows the Unstable FAQ's "form a new single creature" and CR 702.140e. If the pile leaves the battlefield, rulings 6 and 7 apply: each card comes back by itself, and the augment card goes to the graveyard.
  11. A combined creature belongs to the host's owner. You may augment an opponent's host, and that opponent controls the combined creature (ruling on Half-Kitten, Half-). Where a card or rule asks who owns the creature, as mutate does (CR 702.140a), the answer is the host's owner. The augment card joins a creature that was already on the battlefield, and that creature is still the same permanent, as a mutated creature is (CR 731.2c). Each card stays its own owner's card (CR 108.3): when the creature leaves the battlefield, each card goes to its own owner's graveyard, hand, or library (CR 400.3). Official text does not cover a combined creature with two owners; this ruling follows those rules.

MC3. Crafty Octopus and its Contraption deck

Crafty Octopus is the only card in the cube that assembles a Contraption. Contraptions are not drafted. A preset Contraption deck waits in the side pile with the bundles. When you draft Crafty Octopus, take the Contraption deck too.

The Octopus Contraption deck (10 cards): Genetic Recombinator, Duplication Device, Refibrillator, Pet Project, Neural Network, Bee-Bee Gun, Thud-for-Duds, Oaken Power Suit, Arms Depot, Applied Aeronautics. All ten act on creatures.

Contraption rules, from Wizards' Unstable mechanics article:

  1. The Contraption deck is separate from your main deck and does not count toward your 40 cards. In Limited, it has no minimum size and no singleton rule.
  2. At the start of the game, shuffle the Contraption deck and put it face down near your library.
  3. You have three sprockets (1, 2, and 3) and a CRANK! counter that marks the current sprocket.
  4. To assemble a Contraption, turn the top card of your Contraption deck face up and put it onto the battlefield on one of your sprockets. Crafty Octopus assembles one when it enters.
  5. At the beginning of your upkeep, if you control a Contraption, move the CRANK! counter to the next sprocket (1, 2, 3, then 1 again). You may then crank any Contraptions on that sprocket, which triggers their abilities. Cranking is optional.
  6. Contraptions are artifacts. A Contraption that would go to a graveyard goes to your scrapyard instead.
  7. If your Contraption deck is empty when you would assemble, nothing happens. You don't lose the game.

MC4. Big Furry Monster

B.F.M. (Big Furry Monster) is not in the draft. It waits beside the play area, and any player may cast it.

  1. Casting. Any player may cast B.F.M. from outside the game, any time they could cast a creature spell. Take both halves from beside the play area; they are never in your hand. Cast it as one spell: both halves together, with the right half's cost of fifteen black mana ({B}{B}{B}{B}{B}{B}{B}{B}{B}{B}{B}{B}{B}{B}{B}).
  2. Sacrifice toward the cost. As you cast B.F.M., you may sacrifice any number of creatures you control. Each creature sacrificed pays {B} of the cost. Pay the rest with mana.
  3. On the battlefield. B.F.M. is one 99/99 creature made of both cards. It can't be blocked except by three or more creatures. If one half leaves the battlefield, sacrifice the other half (printed text).
  4. Leaving the battlefield. Whenever B.F.M. would go anywhere other than the stack or the battlefield, both halves go back beside the play area instead. That covers dying, exile, being returned to a hand or library, and being countered. Any player may cast it again, for the full cost.

The box holds three B.F.M.s (six cards), one beside each of the three tables.

Cube list

270 draftable slots. Each repeat copy and each Vivid land copy is its own slot. The S.N.O.T. stack is one slot (one card in the packs, the rest in the side pile; see MC1). B.F.M. and the Octopus Contraption deck are not drafted (MC3, MC4).
