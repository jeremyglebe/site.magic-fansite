Creature Feature rules

Local rules · Pegasus Loft

A constructed format with normal Magic gameplay. Deck building is where it differs: decks are packed with creatures, and most of each deck's creatures share one creature type.

Deck construction

  • A deck has at least 60 cards.
  • At least half of the deck must be creature cards, rounded up.
  • At least two thirds of those creature cards must share one creature type, rounded up.
  • Otherwise, normal constructed deck-building rules apply unless the organizer announces something different.

The featured type

Choose one creature type as the deck's featured type. A creature card counts toward the two-thirds requirement if its Oracle creature types include the featured type.

  • A 60-card deck needs at least 30 creature cards.
  • With 30 creature cards, at least 20 must have the featured type.
  • With 31 creature cards, at least 21 must have the featured type.

A creature with several types counts for any one of them. Changelings count as every creature type, unless the organizer has separately banned or restricted that.

Sideboards

If the event uses sideboards, a deck must still meet the creature-count and shared-type requirements after sideboarding.

Banned cards

Creature-type hate

Cards that punish a creature type are banned: any card whose main purpose is to remove, shrink, prevent, or otherwise disadvantage creatures of a named type.

The practical test: if a card singles out a creature type and harms it, or gives protection from it, do not play it. It makes no difference whether the type is printed on the card, as on Tivadar's Crusade (“Destroy all Goblins.”), or chosen or named by a player.

Hate that is not about creature types stays legal unless another rule bans it. That includes color hate, mana-value hate, graveyard hate, and artifact hate.

Temporary type bans

When a creature type wins a Creature Feature tournament, that type is banned for the next four weeks. The ban runs through the same day of the week four weeks after the win, so a type that wins a weekly event sits out the next four events and is legal again at the fifth. Afterward it stays legal until it wins another event.

This keeps dominant types from winning every event, especially those with deep support such as Eldrazi and Slivers.

If the winning deck leans on several creature types, the banned type is the one that satisfied its two-thirds requirement.

The ban covers the featured type only: no deck may choose a banned type as its featured type. Creature cards of that type, changelings included, stay legal in decks built around another type.

Running an event

Before the event, the organizer announces:

  • The permitted card pool. Creature Feature does not adopt any sanctioned format's legality list by default.
  • Any additional house rules.
  • Whether sideboards are used.
  • The creature types currently banned.

During and after the event, the organizer:

  • Keeps a visible list of banned creature types and the date each ban expires.
  • Checks decks for legality before play.
  • Records the winner's declared featured type and the date its four-week ban ends.

Playing and winning

Play normal Magic in whatever structure the event announces, with any house rules the organizer has announced. In an ordinary two-player game, each player starts at 20 life, draws seven cards, and takes normal mulligans, and the starting player skips their first draw. Multiplayer events use normal multiplayer setup. Turn structure, combat, and win and loss conditions are all unchanged; Creature Feature adds no victory condition of its own.

Sources

  • Creature Feature is a local format recorded here first-hand. It is not an official Wizards format and was not adapted from another creature-type format.
