Cube

A cube is a curated pool of a few hundred Magic cards that a group drafts again and again. Instead of opening retail boosters, players draft from packs dealt out of the cube, build decks from what they drafted, play, and then return every card to the cube. Wizards describes Cube as Booster Draft with cards pulled from across Magic's history.

The cube is not a deck. It is a reusable draft environment.

Rulesets

Each page below is a complete draft procedure. Pick one by player count, cube size, and time. All of them share the same deck-building and play rules after the draft.

Ruleset Players Cards used Pool per player How it works
Booster-draft Cube 4–8 45 per player 45 Three 15-card packs each, pick one and pass. The default.
Winston Draft 2 90 30–60 Look at three growing face-down piles; take one or draw blind.
Winchester Draft 2 84 21–63 Take one of four piles; every pile grows each turn.
Grid Draft 2 162 45–54 Take a row or column from a 3×3 grid.
Solomon Draft 2 90 0–90 One player splits a 9-card pack into two piles; the other chooses.
Lawyer Draft 2 128 40 Build five-card hands from a face-up market, with swaps.
Continuous Draft (Three-player) 3 132 44 Four face-up cards split one, two, one.
Continuous Draft (Wizards) Any, in pairs 45 per player 44 Two players merge 44-card pools and redraft in fours; pools carry over to the next opponent.
Three-player Solomon Draft 3 135 0–135 Split, choose, split again; the third player picks from both.
Four-player Grid Draft 4 240 44–48 3×3 grid refilled from a 15-card pack.
Tenchester Draft (Original) 4 360 36 One pick each from a shared 10-card pack.
Tenchester Draft (Revised) 4 360 36 Pass 10-card packs for one pick each; the designer's later version.
Rochester Draft 4–8 45 per player 45 Packs opened face up; picks snake around the table.
Rotisserie Draft 4–8 Whole cube (360) 45 Whole cube face up, one pick at a time.
Team Draft 4–8 45 per player 45 Booster draft with two teams in alternating seats.
Cube Sealed 2–8 90 per player 90 No draft; build from a 90-card pool.
Glimpse Draft 2–8 135 per player 45 Pick one, discard two, pass.
Horde Cube 1–4 162–220 ~45 Solo or co-op Grid Draft against a Horde that drafts too, then a Horde game. Listed under Horde.

Most alternative drafts come from the Cube Draft Format Primer (Reddit, 2016). They were checked against Wizards' articles, the Magic Tournament Rules and other references in the research notes, which settled most of the primer's gaps. Anything still unresolved is listed under “Open question” on the rules page.

Commander cubes have no rules page yet.

What goes into a cube

The designer chooses the cube's themes, power level, colors, archetypes, and restrictions. Before a draft the cube is shuffled and divided into packs, usually of fifteen cards.

Most cubes are singleton: one copy of each card other than basic lands. That is common but not required. Some cubes use duplicates for balance, for theme, or to support a particular archetype, and Wizards' cube articles note that size, restrictions, and structure all vary.

Common cube types

  • Powered cube: high-powered cards, often including extremely efficient spells and fast mana.
  • Unpowered cube: strong cards without the most explosive outliers.
  • Peasant cube: only commons and uncommons.
  • Pauper cube: only commons.
  • Set cube: recreates the feel of drafting one Magic set, often with duplicates.
  • Theme cube: built around a plane, mechanic, creature type, color restriction, story, or custom design goal.
  • Commander cube: drafted for multiplayer Commander-style games, usually with special rules for choosing commanders.

Why build one

A cube lets a group draft the kind of Magic it wants, whenever it wants: a greatest-hits Limited format, a nostalgia set, a custom expansion, a high-powered Vintage draft, a peasant environment, a set cube, a Commander draft box, or something stranger.

For game night, the appeal is that the host can prepare the whole experience in advance:

  • The card pool.
  • The draft structure.
  • The land station.
  • The number of players supported.
  • The intended power level.
  • Any house rules or special draft rules.

Draft structures

The default cube experience mirrors Booster Draft: each player drafts three packs, usually of fifteen cards, and builds a deck of at least forty cards using basic lands supplied separately. The Booster-draft Cube page has the full procedure.

That default is flexible. A smaller group might draft more packs or use one of the other drafts, such as Cube Sealed, Grid, Winston, or Rotisserie. Wizards' guidance treats cube size and structure as design choices and not as one fixed template.

Building a box

A cube box should include:

  • The cube's card pool.
  • Basic lands or a land station.
  • Draft instructions.
  • Player-count guidance.
  • Any custom rules or archetype notes.
  • The tokens, counters, and helper cards the cube needs.
  • A changelog, if the cube evolves over time.

For reusable pack containers, Useful products & tools covers Dragon Shield Cube Shells and decorative Cubeamajigs, with purchase links, capacities, and quantities for an eight-player draft.
