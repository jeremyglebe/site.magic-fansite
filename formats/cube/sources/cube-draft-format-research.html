Cube Draft Formats — Research Notes

Reference notes

These notes record what each outside source says about the alternative cube drafts, and which sources could not be reached. To play, open the matching rules page from the Cube parent page. The primer itself is preserved in the Cube Draft Format Primer source notes.

Checked in November 2026 to settle questions the primer leaves open.

Sources consulted

Source What it covers
Aaron Forsythe, "Winston Draft", Wizards of the Coast, March 2005 Richard Garfield's original Winston rules and a worked example.
Wizards of the Coast, "Casual Formats", August 2008 Official casual write-ups of Solomon, Winston, Rotisserie, Continuous, and Cube drafts.
Wizards of the Coast, Winchester Draft minigame card, Phyrexia: All Will Be One Minigames, 2023 Official Winchester rules as printed on a minigame card.
Magic Judges, MTR 8.5 Team Rochester Draft Tournaments Tournament pick order for Rochester packs, including the double pick at each end.
MTG Wiki: Winston, Winchester, Solomon, Grid, Continuous, Rochester, Rotisserie, Team Limited Secondary summaries, most citing the Wizards articles above.
Riptide Lab forum, "ChannelFireball: Grid Drafting and Tenchester", December 2015 Discussion of Jason Waddell's ChannelFireball article that introduced Tenchester, and of Glimpse drafting.

Sources that could not be reached

  • ChannelFireball (Grid, Tenchester, Rotisserie articles): the site no longer exists.
  • MTGSalvation (Lawyer guide, Rochester basics, Glimpse thread): the site answers automated requests with a Cloudflare bot check.
  • Internet Archive: refuses connections from this repository's build environment, so archived copies of the above could not be read.
  • Wizards' "Limited Alternatives" (2004) and LaPille's "Winchester Draft" (2011): the archived Wizards URLs now return "not found." The Casual Formats page and the Winchester minigame card cover the same formats.

Findings by format

Winston

  • The drafting player looks at each pile without showing it to the opponent (Casual Formats). Forsythe's example has each player keeping drafted cards face down in a pile "that I can peruse at any time."
  • Each player originally supplied three boosters (45 cards) for a 90-card stack. Winston "can be played by up to four" according to MTG Wiki, without a procedure.
  • In the match, the player who drafted second chooses whether to play first (Casual Formats: "Player B gets to choose whether he or she wants to play first during the games").
  • None of the sources says what happens when the stack is empty and a pile needs a card.

Winchester

  • Piles are face up (minigame card; MTG Wiki).
  • The official card uses a single shared stack from six boosters, with four new cards added after each pick, one on each pile including the empty spot. The primer's version has each player feed two cards per turn from a personal 42-card deck. Both add four cards after every pick.
  • "Take turns until all the cards have been drafted" (minigame card), so players keep taking piles after the stack runs out.
  • The card calls for a best-of-three match with a random first player and sideboarding from the rest of the drafted pool.

Solomon

  • Wizards' version splits eight cards at a time from a 90-card stack (the last batch is ten). The primer uses ten 9-card packs.
  • A split of eight and zero is allowed.
  • The chooser takes one pile; the other pile goes to the splitter. Players alternate splitting.
  • The player who did not split first chooses whether to play first in the games.
  • MTG Wiki also describes a four-player Solomon variant (the opener splits each booster into four piles and picks last). It is not part of the primer and is not a rules page here.

Grid

  • MTG Wiki describes the same two-player procedure. With more than two players, "all players except the last are given a full grid," and the last player picks from an incomplete grid. That confirms the four-player reading on the Four-player Grid page.
  • MTG Wiki mentions an optional rule: shuffle the discards together into extra "discard grids," drafted the same way, to need fewer packs.

Continuous

  • Wizards' Continuous Draft is a different format from the primer's three-player version. Each player keeps a 44-card pool from three boosters, pairs with another player, shuffles both pools together, and redrafts in fours (Player A one card, Player B two, Player A the last one), alternating who chooses first. After each best-of-three match, players remove added basic lands and redraft with a new opponent. It has its own page: Continuous Draft (Wizards).
  • The primer's three-player procedure links Wizards' "Limited Alternatives" article, which could not be read, so its origin is unconfirmed.

Tenchester

  • Tenchester was introduced by Jason Waddell in a December 2015 ChannelFireball article (Riptide Lab thread). The primer's "he created an updated version" refers to him.

Rochester

  • MTR 8.5: after the last player takes two cards in a row, drafting reverses back to the player who began. If cards remain, that player also takes two in a row, and drafting reverses again. The pack snakes back and forth until it is empty.
  • The opener ("active player") moves around the table clockwise for the first and third packs and counter-clockwise for the second. The player who opened last in one group of packs opens first in the next.
  • Tournament play gives about 20 seconds to review a pack before the first pick. Players may not review their drafted cards while drafting continues. These are tournament procedures.

Rotisserie

  • Pick order snakes: for players A, B, C and D, the order is A, B, C, D, then D, C, B, A, then A, B, C, D (Casual Formats).
  • Wizards' version has each player place picks face down in front of their seat, and drafting continues until the whole pool is taken.

Team Draft

  • Teams sit alternately, and teammates may not communicate during the draft. Teammates may help each other build, but pools are not shared: each player uses only cards they drafted (MTG Wiki, citing Wizards' "How the Heck Do You Team Draft?").
  • Classic three-versus-three Team Draft plays nine matches, with each player playing each opponent, and every result counts toward the team's score.

Glimpse

  • Riptide Lab posters describe Glimpse drafting as having "burn-1 or burn-2 variants." The primer's version is burn-2 (pick one, discard two).
  • Jason Waddell suggested that in packs 5–9 players burn before picking, to reduce how bomb-heavy decks become. That is a commenter's suggestion, not a rule.
  • None of the reachable sources says whether burned cards are shown.

Lawyer

  • No reachable source beyond the primer. The original MTGSalvation guide could not be read.
