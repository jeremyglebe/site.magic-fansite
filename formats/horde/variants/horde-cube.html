Horde Cube

Rules · Jacob Willson's January 2021 version — draft against the Horde, then fight it

Horde Cube combines a Grid Draft with a Horde game. The Horde "drafts" alongside the players, keeps every card nobody takes, and then plays only the black cards it collected. It works with one to four players, solo or cooperative.

This is a separate ruleset from Classic Zombie Horde. It uses individual life totals and a different victory condition. Do not mix the two without announcing it.

Players and materials

  • 1–4 players, cooperating against the Horde.
  • A Horde Cube. The designer's list is the Horde Cube on Cube Cobra: 300 cards, of which the black cards (including its schemes) are the Horde's and the rest are for the players.
  • A basic land supply for the players.
  • 13 Zombie tokens per player for the Horde deck, as physical cards that can be shuffled into it.
  • One die for the Horde's grid picks (see the table below).
  • Normal-size proxies for any scheme cards in the cube, so they shuffle into the Horde deck.

Grid size by player count

Use a bigger grid for each player beyond the first, so each player ends the draft with about 45 cards.

Players Grid Cards per pack Packs Total cards
1 3×3 9 18 162
2 4×3 12 15 180
3 4×4 16 13 208
4 5×4 20 11 220

Prepare the draft

  1. Shuffle the cube and deal the number of packs in the table, each with the listed number of cards. Return the rest of the cube to its box.
  2. Choose which player picks first in the first pack.

Draft a pack

  1. Lay the pack face up in the grid for your player count.
  2. Give every row and every column its own number.
  3. The Horde picks first: roll the die. The Horde takes the row or column with that number.
  4. Each player then drafts one remaining row or column.
  5. Everything left goes to the Horde, as if it drafted those cards. Nothing is discarded.

Repeat for every pack.

The Horde ends the draft with many more cards than the players. That is intended: it only plays the black ones.

Build the decks

The Horde deck: shuffle together every black card the Horde drafted, every scheme it drafted (schemes have no color, but the designer says they are for the Horde), and 13 Zombie tokens per player. Set its non-black cards aside; they are not used.

Player decks: build normally from your drafted cards and basic lands (at least 40 cards; see Build decks). You may not play black cards or schemes; set any you drafted aside.

The draft's main tension: a black bomb in the grid goes to the Horde at the end of the pack unless a player takes its row or column, even at the cost of a card they wanted.

Setup

  • Each player starts at 20 life.
  • The Horde starts with no hand and no permanents.
  • Players take three turns before the Horde takes its first turn. After that, alternate a Horde turn with the players' turn.

The Horde's turn

  1. Reveal the top card of the Horde deck. If it is a token, put it onto the battlefield and keep revealing.
  2. Stop at the first non-token card and cast it as if the Horde had unlimited mana.
  3. If the Horde controls permanents with activated abilities, it uses each once, before combat.
  4. All the Horde's creatures have haste and attack each turn if able.

When the Horde must make a decision, make it as randomly as possible.

Schemes: the designer says the schemes "are for the horde deck. Just treat them as cards that cost 10 mana essentially." This page reads that as: when the Horde reveals a scheme, it stops revealing and sets the scheme in motion, as it would cast any other non-token card. See the open question below.

More than one player: randomly pick one player each Horde turn, for example by flipping a card. The Horde directs all of its attacks and decisions at that player for the turn.

Damaging and defeating the Horde

  • The Horde has no life total. Whenever a player deals damage to the Horde, mill that many cards from the top of the Horde deck.
  • The players win when the Horde starts its turn with no cards in its deck.
  • A player loses when their life drops from 20 to 0.

Adjusting difficulty

The designer tested 10 and 15 tokens per player and settled on 13. Solo games are very hard, and a second player makes it much easier. Change the token count, or the cards in the cube, to suit your group. Announce the count before play.

Open questions

  • Dice for larger grids: the article uses a six-sided die, which fits the six lines of a 3×3 grid. A 4×3 grid has 7 lines, 4×4 has 8, and 5×4 has 9. Use a die or random method with one outcome per line (for example, reroll results above the number of lines).
  • Player pick order: with two or more players, the article does not say who picks first after the Horde. Rotating the first pick each pack is one option; agree before drafting.
  • Token type: the article says "13 tokens" and "zombie tokens" but gives no power and toughness. Agree before play; 2/2 black Zombie tokens are the usual Horde token.
  • Player turns: in a multiplayer game, the article does not say whether players take one shared turn (as in Classic Horde) or separate turns between Horde turns. Agree before play.
  • Eliminated players: the article does not say what happens when one player reaches 0 life while others survive. Agree whether the remaining players continue.
  • Scheme handling: "treat them as cards that cost 10 mana" leaves open how schemes interact with effects that care about mana value, and whether ongoing schemes stay in play as in Archenemy. Treat them as in the Archenemy scheme rules unless the group agrees otherwise.
  • Black cards: the article does not address multicolored cards that include black. The designer's current list has none.
  • Situations the article does not cover, such as the stack, cards returned to the Horde's hand, or mana taxes: the Classic Zombie Horde page has rulings for these. Agree before play whether to use them.

Optional variants from the article

These are the designer's untested suggestions, not part of the rules above:

  • More than four players: use a traditional booster draft in which the Horde burns (takes) a card from each pack after it is passed.
  • Add to an existing cube: sleeve the Horde's cards differently and shuffle them into another cube. Players may then play black cards from the normal cube.

Sources

The Horde Cube: A Single Player and Co-Op Draft Format, Jacob Willson, Only On Tuesdays, January 19, 2021 (updated June 2024). Card list: The Horde Cube on Cube Cobra. Source notes.
