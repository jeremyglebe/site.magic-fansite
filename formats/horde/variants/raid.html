Horde Raid

Draft · Peter Knudson's Raid variant, February 2013

Raid turns Classic Horde into a staged scenario: the enemy deck is stacked in a fixed order of stages and bosses, artifacts in it become loot for the survivors, and survivors can no longer attack the Horde. The original article calls Raid a work in progress, and it leaves several things for each scenario to decide.

Players and materials

One to four survivors play, with Commander decks or other decks the group agrees on. Prepare an enemy deck divided into named stages (or quests), any boss cards, artifact loot, and the tokens and counters the decks need.

Choose the scenario before play. Classic Horde scales to the player count by randomly removing cards; a staged deck cannot simply do the same, because a random cut can remove a required stage card or boss. A scenario that scales its deck has to say how.

Setup

  1. Put the scenario's stage packets in their required order.
  2. Randomize the cards within each stage, while preserving any specifically positioned boss cards.
  3. Stack the stages so the first stage is drawn first. Do not shuffle the combined library.
  4. Prepare survivor libraries and commanders, draw opening hands, and take normal mulligans.
  5. Unless the scenario changes the original Horde setup, use 20 shared life per survivor and three shared setup turns.
  6. The Horde begins with no hand or permanents. After the setup turns, alternate Horde and survivor turns.

Survivor turns and combat

Survivors take their turns together, with individual libraries, hands, mana, and land plays. They share life and defend together against Horde attacks.

Survivors cannot attack the Horde. This replaces Classic Horde's rule that survivors may attack it to mill its library. The article does not ban noncombat damage or mill effects aimed at the Horde; a scenario that wants to ban those has to say so.

Revealing a wave and finding loot

  1. Reveal the next card from the staged Horde library.
  2. If it is a creature token, add it to the wave and keep revealing.
  3. If it is an artifact, treat it as survivor loot. It does not count as the non-token card that ends the wave, so continue revealing.
  4. Stop on the first other non-token card, or when the library runs out.
  5. Cast the revealed enemy tokens and then the wave's non-token spell, using the normal Horde stack procedure.
  6. Give artifact loot to the survivors using the scenario's declared allocation and timing rule.

The article says artifacts are loot. It does not say who receives them, whether they enter the battlefield or go to a hand, or how Equipment is assigned. A finished Raid box has to supply those rules.

Horde combat and effects

Horde creatures have haste and attack the survivors when able. Survivors can block together. Use the original Horde's unlimited mana and random choices among legal options.

Survivors can respond to Horde spells and abilities. A card returned to the Horde's hand is cast again on its next main phase. A scenario may replace any of this, but has to write out the replacement.

Noncombat damage to the Horde still mills one card per point, as in Classic Horde, unless the scenario changes it. What happens to a boss that is milled this way is not defined.

Stages

The stage order sets the sequence of encounters. Nothing happens automatically between stages: the article adds no battlefield reset and no healing. A scenario that wants either gives its exact trigger and effect.

A wave ends only on a non-token card, as in Classic Horde, so a wave can run from the end of one stage into the start of the next. The article says Raid "plays the same as a normal Horde magic game" apart from its three changes, and none of them stops a wave at a stage boundary or adds a step for announcing a new stage.

Winning and losing

As in Classic Horde, the survivors win by exhausting the enemy deck and defeating its remaining creatures, and the Horde wins when the survivors' shared life total is zero or less. A scenario may replace either with a boss objective or another loss condition, written out in full.

Published scenarios

The Dragonborn

The article's example scenario stacks its deck in this order:

  1. Green cards and tokens.
  2. First dragon boss.
  3. White cards and tokens.
  4. Second dragon boss.
  5. Black cards and tokens.
  6. Red cards and Zombie tokens.
  7. Final boss.

Shuffle within each group. The author mentions a reset between stages, or more sweepers, as possible future tuning; neither is a rule.

Rat Horde

The same article describes a second deck with rules of its own. They apply only to that scenario:

  • When a poison-counter card is revealed, add it to the scenario's poison total. At ten such counters, the survivors lose.
  • Treat a Sorin emblem card as a token flip: it continues the reveal sequence and provides the emblem's continuing creature bonus.
  • A creature returned to the Horde's hand goes directly onto the battlefield.
  • When the Horde has to target something, choose the target at random.

The poison and emblem rules depend on the Rat Horde's own cards.

The article's Rat Horde list, 100 cards, under the author's own group names:

To make this deck harder or easier, the author suggests adding or removing discard spells, or a bigger spell such as another Plague Wind or Barter in Blood.

Open questions

  • The Dragonborn's bosses: which cards in the Dragonborn list are the three bosses, and where does each group end? The list has three Dragon tokens and Hellkite Overlord but labels none of them.
  • Player counts: how many survivors is each scenario for, with what decks, and how does a staged deck scale to the number of survivors? Classic Horde removes cards at random, which could remove a boss, and the article gives no other method.
  • Loot: who gets an artifact revealed as loot, does it go onto the battlefield or into a hand, when does it arrive, and who attaches Equipment? The article says only that the artifact "is considered loot for the survivors to use for themselves."
  • Lost bosses: what happens to a boss that is milled or exiled before the Horde has cast it? Is it gone, like any other card? The article does not say, and noncombat damage can still mill the deck.
